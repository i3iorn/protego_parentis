## Full description (4000 max)

```
Dementor Watch is a screen-time app for a family, built on one idea: the young person being monitored can see everything that is being collected about them.

Most parental control apps are built for the parent and hidden from the child. This one shows both sides the same screens, the same numbers, and the same history. That is not a feature bolted on the side — it is the reason a teenager might agree to have it on their phone at all.


WHAT IT DOES

• A daily allowance, and a bedtime. The two rules most families actually want.
• Categories — group apps together and give the group a shared allowance.
• A limit for a single app, on its own.
• Named schedules: school hours, dinner, homework. Windows with their own rules.
• Ask for more time, with a reason. Answer from your own phone, or set standing rules that answer the small asks for you.
• Bonus minutes, given without being asked.
• A goal worth earning by staying under a target.
• Block apps, or take away just their internet.
• Family-wide web filtering, and a browser that can be locked down.
• Saved places, and a message when the phone arrives at one or leaves.
• Ask where the phone is — and the phone always says that you asked.
• Lock the screen now, from a parent's phone.
• An emergency button the young person can always reach, whatever else is blocked.
• Short messages between the phones. Text and emoji only.
• Join a family by scanning a code, by finding the other phone on your Wi-Fi, or by typing the code in.


WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

A screen called "What leaves this phone" that lists, in plain language, every single thing that is measured, every single thing that is sent, and everything that is never collected at all. It cannot be hidden and it cannot be switched off.

A shared history. Every rule change, every request, every answer, every time someone asked where the phone was — with who did it. Both sides read the same list. It is hash-chained, so an entry cannot be quietly removed.

Some things are deliberately not configurable. A young person is always told when they have been located and always told when a parent has locked their phone. There is no setting to turn those off, because an app that can be configured to watch someone silently is a different app.


PRIVACY

There is no account, no sign-up, and no server of ours. We receive nothing, ever.

Phones in a family talk to each other through a relay you choose — by default a public one, or your own if you prefer. Everything is encrypted on the sending phone first, so the relay carries sealed messages it cannot read. Each phone signs what it sends with a key generated inside its own hardware keystore.

No advertising. No analytics. No crash reporting. Nothing in this app reports back to anyone, and nothing is sold or shared. The only libraries it uses are ones that draw and read a QR code, on the device.

Your messages, photos, keystrokes and screen contents are not read, not stored and not sent. The app has no access to them and asks for no permission that would give it access.


WHAT YOU NEED

Screen-time measurement needs Android's usage access, granted once in Settings.

Full enforcement — actually blocking apps rather than only measuring them — needs the phone to be set up as a managed device. That means a factory reset and setting it up with Dementor Watch as the device owner, which is a decision to make together rather than one to spring on someone. Without it the app measures and reports honestly, and blocks nothing.

Location features are optional and off unless you turn them on.

Everything can be handed back. "Release this phone" lifts every restriction and gives up control.
```
