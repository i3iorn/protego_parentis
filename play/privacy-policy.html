Dementor Watch — Privacy Policy

Last updated {{DATE}} · Applies to Dementor Watch version 1.0.0 and later

Dementor Watch is a screen-time app for a family. It runs on a young person's phone and on their parents' phones. It is built on the principle that the person being monitored can see exactly what is being collected about them, so this document says the same things the app's own “What leaves this phone” screen says — in the same detail.

Who is responsible

{{DEVELOPER NAME}}, contact {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}.

We do not operate a server and we do not receive your data. There is no Dementor Watch account, no sign-up and no back end belonging to us. Nothing in this app sends anything to the developer, at any time, for any reason.

What is measured on the phone

  • Which apps are opened and for how long.
  • Total screen time per day.
  • Which of the family's rules applied, and when.

All of this is worked out on the device and stored on the device. It is readable by the person using that phone, in the app, at any time.

What is never collected

The app does not read, store or transmit: messages, photos, keystrokes, screen contents, call logs, contacts, browsing history within a site, or the contents of any other app. It has no access to any of these and asks for no permission that would give it access.

What is sent to the family's other phones

Only what the family has switched on. Depending on the settings, that can be:

  • Requests and answers — how many minutes were asked for, what for, the reason the young person wrote, and whether the answer was yes or no.
  • A daily summary — today's total, how much of the limit is left, and the five most-used apps with how long each was open. No timestamps and no history.
  • Location, only on request — a single position, sent when a parent asks for it or when the young person raises an emergency. The young person is notified every single time it is asked for, and every request is written into the shared history both sides can read. No trail is kept: a parent's phone holds one position, the most recent, with no history behind it.
  • Arrivals and departures — the name of a saved place (“Arrived at School”) and no coordinates at all.
  • Sites reached, if counting is switched on — the domain names looked up today and how many times each. No addresses within a site, no times of day, nothing about page contents. Resets daily; no history is kept on either phone.
  • Messages — text and emoji typed in the app's Messages screen. Pictures, files and attachments cannot be sent; the app has no mechanism for it.
  • Device health — whether the phone is still checking in and its battery level.

How it travels, and who can read it

Every message is encrypted on the sending phone with AES-256-GCM, using a key derived from a passphrase the family chooses and that only the family's phones hold. Each phone also signs its messages with a private key generated inside the device's hardware-backed keystore, which cannot be exported from it.

Messages travel through a public relay — by default ntfy.sh, operated by a third party, and changeable to any server the family prefers, including one they run themselves. The relay carries ciphertext and cannot read any of it. It is a postbox, not a recipient. Its operator's own privacy policy governs whatever connection metadata it keeps; we have no arrangement with them and no access to it.

If the family has not connected any parent phone, the app contacts no server at all.

Pairing over your local network

When adding a phone to a family, the two phones can find each other on the same Wi-Fi instead of one code being typed. While — and only while — one of those screens is open, a phone announces on the local network that it is offering to pair. The announcement contains nothing about your family: no topic, no server, no name, no passphrase. It says only that a pairing is in progress, and it stops after two minutes or when the screen closes, whichever comes first.

The family code itself is sent directly from one phone to the other, over the local network, and only after a person has compared a code shown on both screens and agreed. The passphrase is never sent — not here and not anywhere. It is typed on each phone.

Nothing leaves your network during this, and no server of any kind is involved. A code sent this way travels in the clear on the local network, so it is worth doing on a network you trust rather than a public one; the app says so on the screen.

Children's data

This app is installed on a young person's phone by their parent or guardian, who sets it up and consents on their behalf. It is designed so that the young person can see everything that is collected about them and everything that is sent:

  • The “What leaves this phone” screen states every category, in full, and cannot be hidden or switched off.
  • The shared history records every rule change, every request, every answer and every location request, with who made it, and is readable by the young person as well as by the parents.
  • Notifications telling the young person that they were located, or that a parent locked the phone, cannot be silenced.

No profile is built, no data is sold or shared with anyone, and nothing is used for advertising. There is no advertising in this app.

Third parties

None. The app contains no analytics, no crash reporting service, no advertising SDK and no social SDK. Its only network connection is to the relay the family chose, carrying encrypted family messages.

How long data is kept, and how to delete it

  • Screen-time records and the shared history stay on the phone that made them. Uninstalling the app removes them.
  • Messages: the most recent few hundred, on each phone. Either side can clear their own copy from the Messages screen at any time.
  • Leaving a family, from the family connection screen, erases everything that arrived from the other phones — positions, arrivals, sites, requests, device keys and names.
  • Relayed messages expire on the relay according to its own retention, and are unreadable there in any case.

There is no account to delete, because there is no account.

Permissions, and why each one exists

  • Usage access — to measure screen time. This is the app's core function and it cannot work without it.
  • Location, including in the background — to answer a parent's explicit request for a position, to notice arrivals at saved places, and to apply rules that depend on where the phone is. The young person is notified of every position request. No coordinates leave the phone for any other reason.
  • See all installed apps — to show the family which apps are on the phone, apply per-app limits, and flag newly installed apps. The list never leaves the device.
  • Camera — used for one thing: reading a family code off another phone's screen when joining. Frames are decoded in memory and discarded. Nothing is photographed, saved or sent, and the permission is only requested when that screen is opened.
  • VPN — used only to withhold internet from specific apps the family has blocked. It routes nothing anywhere: blocked traffic is dropped on the device. No traffic is inspected, logged or forwarded.
  • Device administration / device owner — to apply the limits the family set: suspending apps, locking the screen when asked, and preventing the app being removed or the rules bypassed.
  • Notifications, boot completion, foreground service — to warn before time runs out, and to keep measuring after a restart.
